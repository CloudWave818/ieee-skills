# IEEE Reviewer Criteria

Use this reference for reviewer simulation and pre-submission checks.

## First Impression

Reviewers often form an early impression from formatting, figures, tables, notation, and writing quality. Flag issues that suggest low rigor:

1. Blurry, pixelated, or hand-drawn-looking figures.
2. Overlapping legends or unreadable line styles.
3. Missing units, scales, or axis labels.
4. Tables with inconsistent precision or unclear best/second-best marking.
5. Figure captions that do not explain variables or experimental conditions.
6. Page breaks that separate figures, captions, tables, and titles awkwardly.
7. Inconsistent notation, abbreviations, and capitalization.

## Technical Review Dimensions

Assess:

1. Novelty: Is the contribution more than a routine combination?
2. Problem importance: Is the condition or engineering harm convincing?
3. Technical soundness: Are assumptions, derivations, algorithms, and proofs correct?
4. Method rationale: Are design choices tied to system characteristics?
5. Experimental adequacy: Are baselines, ablations, and metrics sufficient?
6. Fair comparison: Are datasets, preprocessing, tuning, and hardware comparable?
7. Reproducibility: Are implementation details enough for replication?
8. Clarity: Can a technical reader understand and verify the work?

## Decision Risk

Classify risks:

Critical: likely rejection unless fixed. Examples: missing baselines, unsupported main claim, incorrect derivation, no real experiment for the claimed condition.

Major: likely major revision. Examples: weak ablation, unclear method rationale, insufficient discussion, missing complexity analysis.

Minor: likely minor revision. Examples: typo, inconsistent formatting, unclear caption, missing abbreviation definition.

## Review Output

When reviewing, lead with findings. For each finding include:

1. Severity.
2. Location if known.
3. Problem.
4. Why it matters for IEEE review.
5. Concrete fix.

Do not only praise. If information is missing, state the assumption.
